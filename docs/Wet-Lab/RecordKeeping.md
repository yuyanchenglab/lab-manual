## LabArchives — Daily Lab Records
All wet lab members are required to keep an updated digital lab notebook on LabArchives for daily reports of results, protocols, and general record-keeping. You get access as part of [Lab Onboarding](../Onboarding/LabOnboarding).

When you get your digital Lab Archives notebook from Yuyan it should include sample folders for organization structure. This structure can be flexible, however, **your notebook needs to be navigable and readable by your future self and others**
It is expected that you update your lab notebook daily. Include enough detail that someone with little context could understand and reproduce your work! 

Example Lab Archives Structure:
* **Daily Notes:** Weekly plans, daily to-do lists, etc. (Daily Notes >> Year >> Month >> Entry for each week)
* **Projects:** Experimental records for specific projects (Projects >> project id/name >> Experiment >> etc >> Entry with title (YYMMDD_description)
* **Collaboration:** Experimental records specifically for projects that are collaborations with other labs
* **Protocols**
* **Meeting Notes**
* Any other helpful folders for example Grants, Colony Management, etc. 

## Box — Raw & Aggregated Files
The lab has a Box account for aggregated/summarized/presentable project data and metadata (as opposed to GitHub, which is for the dry lab's raw project results and code — see [Dry Lab](../Dry-Lab/ProjectSetup)). 

Files should be saved to a respective projects specific folder. These are typically managed and organized by Yuyan. For easy navigation, files and folders can be added to "Collections" on the online Box platform. 
The lab Box also includes folders for Lab Management related protocols and documents.

## Reproducibility
Maintaining a laboratory notebook is **essential to scientific rigor and reproducibility.** 

Your lab records should be clear enough that someone with minimal context could accurately interpret your plans, data, and conclusions.
Moreover, your lab records need to be detailed enough that another lab member or research could reproduce your results and conclusions.

As summarized by the BGS Responsible Conduct of Research page on [Acquisition and Management of Data](https://www.med.upenn.edu/bgs-rcr-exdes/case-study-module-1.html): 

"The laboratory notebook provides a record of primary and secondary data. It can, and should, provide as well a record of collaboration, interpretation, and decisions. It allows authentication of work by outside parties and proves ownership in claims to discovery. The notebook often represents the critical bridge between different studies occurring at different times in the lab, allowing researchers to identify, replicate, and build upon previous findings – including both positive and negative results. Scientific rigor, reproducibility, and progress all depend critically on one’s ability to maintain an appropriate and effective laboratory notebook."
